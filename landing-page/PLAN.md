## 2. What the page is for

The current site sells financial planning to individuals and families. The SOEF site sells a program to veteran business owners. The new page does neither. It is the firm's page of record.

Who lands on it, in my estimate (no traffic data was available to me):

- A veteran business owner who saw "Plan For Your Goals, LLC" in a SOEF disclosure and wants to know who that is.
- A CPA, attorney or referral partner checking you out before an introduction.
- An existing client looking for the login.
- Someone following a link from your Form ADV, the SEC adviser lookup, an old email or an old search result.

All four are checking, not shopping. So the page answers three questions in order: who is this, how is he paid, where do I go next.

## 4. Why it is built this way

### Business side (billion-dollar brain trust, your own practice)

- **Narrowing is the right move.** The old site speaks to everyone with money questions. Your chosen market is veteran-owned service companies. Thirty-three pages and 42 blog posts aimed at a general audience pull attention away from that market. One page ends the split.
- **This page is not a rung on your ladder.** Your ladder starts at SOEF (Readiness Review, then VBO Lab, then a track). This page is the detour a careful buyer takes to check the firm, and its job is to send them back to the ladder with more confidence.
- **Certainty comes from standing, not claims.** A skeptical owner needs to believe in the program, in you and in the firm. This page handles the last two with things that can be checked: registration, CRD numbers, the brochure, an address, and a plain statement of how you are paid. No results, no testimonials, no promises.
- **One action, measured.** One button, repeated, each copy tagged so SOEF's analytics show how many visitors arrive from the firm page and from which spot. The side note is a text link so it does not compete.
- **Less to maintain.** One page on the platform you already run replaces a WordPress site someone else controls.

Two trade-offs, named:

- The one-action rule against serving individuals. Resolved by your answer: individuals get a sentence, not a button.
- Retiring the $195 consultation and the Fit Guide ends that route for new individual clients. I could not see whether that route currently produces clients. If it does, that is revenue this plan switches off. See risks.

### Client side (advisor brain trust)

This is a content call, so only the compliance lens applies. Two standing rules were kept: the page lists household security before business value before exit, and it uses your product names exactly as your disclosure file records them.

## 6. Hosting and DNS

### One Squarespace fact that shapes the choice

A Squarespace site can hold several domains, but only one is primary and every other domain redirects to it. This comes from Squarespace's help pages as summarised in a web search; I could not open the pages themselves, so confirm it in your Domains panel.

That gives two ways to do this.

| | Option A: its own small Squarespace site (recommended) | Option B: a page on the SOEF site |
|---|---|---|
| What the visitor sees | planforyourgoals.com stays in the address bar | planforyourgoals.com jumps to a page under secureoneveryfront.com |
| Cost | A second Squarespace subscription | None |
| Header and footer | The firm's own | SOEF's, unless the page hides them |
| Fit with your rule that SOEF is a brand and the firm is the adviser | Clean. The firm's address shows the firm's page | Weaker. The firm's web address resolves to the brand's site |
| Privacy Policy and Terms | Stay at their current addresses | Move under the SOEF domain |

I recommended Option A and you chose it on 2026-11-19. The firm's web address is on your Form ADV and in years of emails and documents. It should keep answering as the firm.

### Checklist for the conversation with your developer

Ask for these, in this order.

1. **A full export of the current DNS records** for planforyourgoals.com, every record, before anything changes.
2. **Where the domain is registered and who holds the login.** Ask for the domain to be moved into an account you control, or for full access to it.
3. **Leave every email record untouched.** That means the MX records, the SPF and DMARC text records, the DKIM records and autodiscover. Only the two website records change (the root record and `www`). Your firm profile says the firm's mail runs on Microsoft 365 and is archived by Comply. If mail stops, the archive has a gap, and that is a books-and-records problem as well as an inconvenience.
4. **Any subdomains in use.** Ask for a list.
5. **A full backup of the WordPress site**, files and database, handed to you.
6. **Keep the WordPress hosting alive for 30 to 60 days** after the switch so you can go back.
7. **Whether planforyourgoals.link is involved.** It is a separate domain and should be unaffected, but confirm who runs it and where its links point.
8. **Lower the DNS time-to-live a day or two before the switch** so a rollback takes minutes, not hours.

Squarespace shows the exact records to enter in its Domains panel when you connect the domain. Use those; I have not listed values here because I could not verify them.

## 8. Measurement

- **The number that matters:** visits to SOEF that arrive from the firm page. Each button is tagged (`utm_source=planforyourgoals.com`, `utm_medium=referral`, `utm_campaign=firm-page`, and `utm_content` of header, hero, program or footer).
- **Secondary:** client-login clicks and the two contact links.
- **When to look:** at 30 and 90 days. If almost no one clicks through, the page is doing its record-keeping job but not its routing job, and the hero wording is the first thing to test.
